Affect Coordinates
A colour-based exhibition of image atmosphere

Affect Coordinates explores how hue, saturation and brightness
can place images on a Valence–Arousal map.
Valence describes how unpleasant or pleasant an image may feel;
arousal describes how calm or intense it may feel.
The map is not a final judgement of emotion,
but a colour-based reading open to human interpretation.

Valence

Valence describes how positive or negative an image may feel. In this project, lower values suggest a more uncomfortable, heavy or negative atmosphere, while higher values suggest a more pleasant, luminous or positive atmosphere.

〰

Arousal

Arousal describes how calm or intense an image may feel. In this project, lower values suggest stillness or softness, while higher values suggest intensity, energy, or visual stimulation.

◌

Hue describes the dominant colour tendency of an image, such as red, blue, or green.

✺

Saturation describes how vivid or muted the colours are. Higher saturation may contribute to stronger visual energy.

Brightness

Brightness describes how light or dark the image appears. Brighter images may contribute to a more open affective reading.

From colour to coordinates

Formula and Calibration

This formula is a transparent way of turning colour features into map positions. It is based on the idea that images can feel more positive or negative, and more calm or intense. It also draws on colour-emotion research showing that brightness, saturation, and hue can influence how an image feels.

V_model and A_model are informed by Valence–Arousal theory and colour-emotion research (Wilms & Oberfeld, 2018).

V_model = 0.70 × B + 0.30 × S

A_model = 0.60 × S + 0.20 × B

+ 0.15 × (R × S) − 0.05 × (Bl × S)

V_cal = 0.34 + 0.45 × V_model

A_cal = 0.17 + 0.86 × A_model

B = brightness mean · S = saturation mean · R = red ratio · Bl = blue ratio.
V_model and A_model are initial model estimates; V_cal and A_cal are calibrated map coordinates.

Interpretation remains open

Theory and Limits

Colour is only one doorway into an image. This map makes a colour-based reading visible, but it does not close interpretation. Image atmosphere can also be shaped by subject matter, composition, memory and context.

Theory and Limits

Why Interpretation Remains Open

This project draws on colour-affect research suggesting that hue, saturation and brightness can influence perceived valence and arousal. For example, Wilms and Oberfeld show that brighter and more saturated colours tend to be associated with higher arousal and more positive affective responses (Wilms and Oberfeld, 2018).

However, these relationships are not treated as fixed emotional rules. Image atmosphere also depends on subject matter, composition, cultural context and the viewer’s own experience. Following Lin’s discussion of digital curatorial interpretation, the interface is therefore designed to make interpretation visible and open to discussion rather than final (Lin, 2024).

The map works as a transparent and reviewable interpretive layer: it places modelled readings, author interpretation and audience responses in relation to one another. The Ambiguous Readings section shows where the colour-based model becomes limited, unstable or open to interpretation.

Data Use and Participation Terms

  • Your ratings are anonymous and will only be used for this dissertation project.

Welcome to Affect Coordinates. This project explores how colour features in images can be translated into emotional map positions.

The map uses two dimensions. Valence describes how unpleasant or pleasant an image may feel. Arousal describes how calm or intense an image may feel.

The project uses hue, saturation, and brightness to place images on this map. However, the map is not a final judgement of emotion. It is a way to make a colour-based computational reading visible, so that it can be compared with human interpretation.

What this project is about

Affect Coordinates explores how colour features in images can be translated into emotional map positions. Each image is placed on a Valence–Arousal map using hue, saturation, and brightness.

Valence means how unpleasant or pleasant an image may feel. Arousal means how calm or intense an image may feel. The map is not a final judgement of emotion. It offers a colour-based reading that can be compared with human interpretation.
